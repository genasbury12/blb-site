where? Sound familiar?

You've done the work. But where are the customers?

You didn't start blogging because you wanted another website to maintain. You started because you wanted to make money doing something that was yours. Maybe you wanted an extra $1,000 a month. Maybe you wanted to work from home instead of spending your days at a job that leaves you exhausted. Maybe you wanted to create digital products, help women who have gone through something you've experienced, or build a business around something you genuinely love.

So you did what every blogging tutorial told you to do.

Picked a nicheBought a domainSet up a websiteKeywords + SEOPinterestAffiliate marketingEmail lists

And you kept going. Even when it got frustrating. Even when nobody commented. Even when the traffic wasn't growing as quickly as you'd hoped. Even when your bank account showed absolutely nothing for the hours you'd invested. Because somewhere in the back of your mind, you believed that if you just kept publishing, eventually the money would come.

But what if that's the very assumption keeping you stuck?

sell what? The missing job

More blog posts won't automatically fix a business that has nothing to sell.

Think about the last month you worked on your blog. How many hours did you spend creating content? Improving your website? How many times did you rearrange your content calendar, research a new platform, or download another template? Now ask yourself something different.

How much time did you spend building something a customer could actually buy?

Not something you might sell someday. Not an idea sitting in a notebook. Something real. Something that solves a specific problem for a specific person. Something with a sales page that explains why it's worth paying for.

Because if a woman discovers your blog tomorrow, reads three articles, and absolutely loves what you teach, what happens next? Can she buy something from you? Does she know what that something is? Is there a reason she'd want it? Or does she leave your website, and all you're left with is another pageview?

Getting someone to your blog and giving her a reason to buy from your business are two completely different jobs. And you need both.

Picture it

Imagine finally knowing exactly what you're building.

Not waking up Monday morning wondering whether you should work on Pinterest or write another article. Not watching tutorials because you're afraid you're missing something. Not abandoning another digital product halfway through.

Imagine opening your laptop and knowing:

  • This is the person my business helps.
  • This is the problem she wants solved.
  • This is what I'm selling her.
  • This is how she'll discover it.
  • And this is what happens when she's ready to buy.

You have a sales page you're proud to share. A free resource that introduces the right people to your business. Welcome emails that keep the conversation going. A blog filled with content that has a purpose. And a plan for getting your offer in front of people instead of hoping someone eventually finds it.

Would everything be perfect? Of course not. Would you still have marketing to do, things to test, and skills to improve? Absolutely. But you'd finally have a business you could work on improving. Not just another collection of unfinished ideas.

since 2017
My story

I know what happens when you put blogging before the business.

Because I did it for two years. I started my first lifestyle blog in 2017. I had an old MacBook, a lot of ambition, and what I thought was a perfectly reasonable plan. I'd write blog posts, get traffic, use affiliate links, and eventually make money. So that's what I worked on. Blog posts. Pinterest pins. Affiliate links. Display ads. All the things I was told successful bloggers did.

And after roughly two years? I'd earned about $50 in affiliate commissions. The problem was that I needed $100 to cash out. So I hadn't even been paid. I also created a planner and sent it to my email list. It made exactly $0.

I wasn't sitting around waiting for something to happen. I was working. A lot. But I hadn't understood what it meant to build a business around the content I was creating.

In early 2020, I spent my last $200 on a blog coach. And I started doing something different. I learned to think about the customer before the content. The offer before the traffic. The message before the website. The business before the blog.

On April 16, 2020, I received my first Stripe payout. $18.52. It wasn't a fortune. But I still remember it because I had finally sold something through the business I was building. Within approximately three to four months of changing my approach, I brought in around $4,000. Over the years that followed, I grew Boss Lady Bloggers into a multi six figure business through coaching, website design, and digital education.

Your blog isn't your business. Your blog is how you market your business.

My results are my own, and they aren't a prediction of what anyone else will earn. But the business building lessons behind them are exactly what I want to teach you.

your path One system. Two starting paths.

You're not starting at the same place as every other blogger.

Some women haven't even chosen a domain yet. Others have dozens of articles, an email list, digital products, and years of work behind them. So why would they need the exact same starting checklist?

Inside BOSS, you'll begin with a 30 question Business Audit. You'll receive a recommended starting path, your top three priorities, and a personalized six month roadmap. Then you'll follow the path that fits where you are.

Path one

Build From Scratch

For the woman who wants to start a business but doesn't know what to sell, whom to help, or what to work on first.

You'll work through choosing your audience, identifying a problem, developing an offer, clarifying your message, setting up your website and email system, and creating marketing that supports the business.

Path two

Optimize & Automate

For the woman who already has a blog, content, and maybe a product or two, but isn't getting the sales or clarity she expected.

You'll examine what exists, identify gaps, improve your offer and message, strengthen your customer journey, and build systems that make the business easier to run.

Either way, you're not trying to build everything at once. You're focusing on the pieces that matter for your stage.

Is BOSS right for you?

Let's be honest about fit.

You'll probably love this program if:

  • You want to build a real business using your blog as marketing.
  • You want to create and sell your own digital products, services, or other offers.
  • You're tired of having lots of blogging knowledge but no clear business direction.
  • You want a structured plan instead of another random collection of tips.
  • You're willing to test your ideas, make decisions, and implement what you learn.
  • You want to use AI and systems to make your business easier to manage.
  • You want help building a business that fits your actual life.

This probably isn't for you if:

  • You're happy keeping your blog purely as a hobby.
  • You only want to pursue pageviews, ads, or affiliate commissions.
  • You're looking for guaranteed income.
  • You're unwilling to do the implementation required for your chosen level.
  • You want someone to take over the business without involving you in how it works.

You don't have to know everything before joining. But you do have to want to build something.
