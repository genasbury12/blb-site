If you only do one thing

Start with the 5-Minute Money Reality Check.

Twelve resources landing in your inbox at once is a lot, so let me tell you where to begin. This one is a single page, it takes five minutes, and it is the thing I wish someone had handed me when we were $34,000 in the hole and I was too scared to open the bank app.

  1. Write down what comes in. Every dollar, from every source, for one normal month. No rounding up to make it look better.
  2. Check the Big Four. Housing, transport, food, utilities. This is usually where the whole month quietly disappears.
  3. Find the quiet leaks. Subscriptions, convenience runs, small yeses. Not to shame you. Just so you can see them.
  4. Get your Reality Number. Simple subtraction, then the one next step that makes the biggest difference for your family.

Then work through the rest of the library at whatever pace suits your life.

Before you sign up.

Are all twelve really free, or is this a sample of something paid?

All twelve, really free, in full. Not watered-down versions of paid products. No credit card, no trial that quietly starts billing you. I do sell a membership elsewhere on the site and I will mention it sometimes, but this library is yours whether you ever buy anything or not. Plenty of families never do, and that is completely fine by me.

Do I have to use all twelve? That feels like a lot.

Not even slightly. Most people use two or three and ignore the rest, and that is exactly how it should work. Start with the Reality Check, then pick whatever is bothering you most this week. The others will still be there in six months if you need them.

What are you going to do with my email address?

Send you the library, then write to you about once a week. That is it. I do not sell it, rent it, or share it with anyone, ever. One click unsubscribes you and I will not chase you about it.

I am honestly scared to look at my numbers.

I know. I avoided ours for years, because I already knew opening the bank app would ruin my day. Here is what I did not expect: seeing it written down was so much less frightening than the version I had built up in my head. The not-knowing is the heavy part.

You are not bad with money. You were never taught. Those are two completely different things, and this is a no-shame space. Take the five minutes.

Will these work if our income changes every month?

Yes, and it might matter more for you than for anyone. Use a normal month, or if there is no such thing, use your lowest recent month. Building the plan on the lean months instead of the good ones is exactly how you stop getting caught out.

Do I need a printer or any special software?

Neither. They are PDFs. Print them if you like writing by hand, or open them on your phone and fill them in there. I built them to work at a kitchen table with a pen, because that is where I built them.
