How to Start a Money-Making Blog in 2026 (the right order)

save this one, you'll need it
by Genasys ✦ January 14, 2026 ✦ 12 min read

Here's the truth nobody tells you: most new bloggers don't fail because they're bad at blogging. They fail because they do everything in the wrong order — design first, niche last, offer never.

After coaching 300+ women through blog launches, I can tell you the order matters more than the effort. This post walks you through the exact sequence I use with every student, so you can skip the six months of spinning.

01. Decide what your blog is for

Before fonts, before names, before anything — decide what this blog is supposed to do for your life. A hobby blog and a business blog are built completely differently. If you want income, you're building a business that publishes a blog, and that one sentence changes every decision after it.

"You're not starting a blog. You're starting a business that happens to have a blog."

02. Pick a niche your future offer lives in

Don't pick a niche by what you could write about forever. Pick it by who you want to help and what they'd happily pay to fix. Your niche isn't a topic — it's a person with a problem.

  • Bad niche test: "I love talking about this."
  • Better niche test: "I can name the person, the problem, and what solving it is worth to her."
  • Best niche test: she's already trying to solve it — badly — with free scraps.
Boss Lady Tip

Stuck choosing? Take the free "Start a Blog the Right Way" course in the resource library — Module 1 walks you through this exact decision with worksheets.

03. Set up the simple version of your blog

One homepage. One about page. One way to join your email list. That's a launch. The 40-page website with a custom logo can come after your first readers do — not before. Perfection at this stage is just procrastination wearing lip gloss.

simple, published & growing beats perfect & invisible

04. Write content that leads somewhere

Every post should move a reader one step closer to your future offer. Before you write anything, ask: "If she loves this post, what should she want next?" If the answer is "nothing," the post is a dead end — rewrite the idea, not just the headline.

05. Make your first tiny offer sooner than feels comfortable

You don't need 10,000 readers to make your first sale — you need one clear promise for one specific person. A mini-course, a template pack, a power hour. Small, specific and priced honestly. The confidence comes after the first sale, never before it.

That's the order: purpose, niche, simple setup, pointed content, tiny offer. Everything else — the branding, the fancy funnels, the aesthetic feed — is decoration on top of this foundation.
