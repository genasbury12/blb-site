The real goal

Let's talk about what you actually want.

not the pageviews, babe. the real thing.

Not the blog stats. Not the pageviews. The real thing underneath all of it.

You want to open your laptop and see a website that looks like you. The colors, the fonts, the whole feel of it. Something you'd screenshot. Something you'd send to your group chat without adding "ignore how it looks, I'm still working on it."

You want someone to ask what you do, and to answer in one clean sentence instead of trailing off into "well, it's kind of a blog, but I'm also trying to..."

You want the notification. The one that says somebody bought something you made, at 9pm on a Tuesday, while you were doing absolutely nothing.

You want the hours you're pouring into this to show up somewhere. In your bank account. In your calendar. In how you feel on a Sunday night.

And you want it soon. Not in three years, once the traffic finally builds. Soon.

That's not too much to ask for. It's just usually asked for in the wrong order.

the women, the builds, the little life behind it
My story

I wanted the exact same thing, and I got it wrong for two years.

Back in 2017 I was living in my aunt's basement, googling "how to make money online," and blogging is what came back.

So I did what everybody says to do. WordPress. A template I picked because it was pretty. A lifestyle blog. Post after post after post, affiliate links tucked in, ads switched on, refreshing the dashboard like something was going to change.

Two years of that. I made nothing.

And honestly? I cried about it more than once. Not really about the money. About what it seemed to mean. That I was wasting my time. That working from home was something other women got to do and I didn't.

Then I stopped trying to be a blogger, and started building a business that happened to use a blog. I made my own offer. I learned how to design and write a site built to sell instead of built to look nice. And I rebuilt everything in a completely different order: what I was selling first, then the words, then the design.

Four months later, I made $4,000.

The part I still think about is the timeline. Two years of nothing. Four months of something. Same person, same effort, different order.

The part nobody tells you

So here's what I wish somebody had told me in year one.

01

Your blog isn't the business. It's the marketing.

The blog is how people find you and decide they like you. The offer is how you get paid. When you build the blog without the offer, you've built a beautiful machine pointing at nothing.

02

Ads and affiliates pay in pennies, and pennies need crowds.

Both need a huge amount of traffic before they turn into money you'd actually notice. And traffic is harder to build now than it's ever been. Keep affiliate links if you like them. I'd skip ads completely.

03

Most "how to start a blog" articles exist to sell you hosting.

That's the business model. The same advice gets recycled from post to post, mostly written by people who aren't actually making money from their own blogs. You weren't behind. You were following a map drawn by people paid to point you down that road.

The order

Almost everyone builds in exactly the wrong order.

not more effort. a different order.
What most people do

Pick a template, build the site, write content, chase traffic, and then eventually try to figure out how to make money from any of it.

What actually works

Decide what you're selling. Write the copy that sells it. Then build the site around that copy. A template can't do that part for you, and WordPress can't either. Publishing and selling are two different jobs.
