then what? The real problem

You don't need another 50 blog posts. You need a way to turn readers into buyers.

Let's say you publish an amazing blog post tomorrow. It starts ranking on Google. People find it. They read it. They love it. And then what?

Do they know you sell something that could help them? Does your website give them a reason to explore your offer? Do you have a way to stay connected if they're not ready to buy? Or do they leave and never hear from you again?

This is the problem I see with so much blogging advice. It teaches you how to attract readers. But it rarely teaches you what to do with those readers once they arrive. You're told to focus on traffic. Grow your email list. Write more content. Be patient. And eventually, the money will come.

Except traffic doesn't automatically create sales. Neither does an email list. And neither does having a beautiful website. You need something worth buying, a reason people want it, and a clear process that connects your audience to your offer. That's what I want to help you build.

the method The Blog Profit Method

My 6 part process for turning a blog into a business that sells.

Most bloggers have pieces of a business. Content. A website. Maybe an email list. Some even have a digital product or service. But the pieces aren't working together. And when nothing is connected, every sale feels like a mystery. That's why I teach a specific sales process.

1

Find your money opportunity

First, we figure out what people should actually be paying you for.

If you don't have an offer, we'll explore your audience's problems, your expertise, and potential opportunities. If you already have something to sell, we'll examine why it isn't producing the sales you want.

  • Identifying the customers you want to attract
  • Understanding what they're struggling with
  • Finding problems they're willing to pay to solve
  • Choosing or improving your paid offer
  • Writing a clear, compelling offer promise
  • Positioning your offer so the right people understand its value
You'll leave this stage with a clear offer strategy and a reason behind it.
2

Build your front end sales process

Now we make sure people who discover your blog can also discover what you sell.

Think about the last time you landed on someone's website, read something helpful, and immediately wanted to know more about working with them. That's what we want to make possible.

  • How people currently find your blog
  • Which content attracts potential customers
  • How your website introduces your offers
  • Where calls to action should appear
  • How your blog posts can naturally lead to paid solutions
  • What your sales pages need to communicate
  • Which parts of your current buying process need improvement
You'll have a clear path connecting your content to your paid offers.
3

Build your back end sales process

Because not everyone will buy the first time they visit your website.

Some people will need to get to know you. Some will have questions. Some will need more information before they're comfortable purchasing. And some simply won't be ready yet. So what happens after they leave your blog?

  • Creating a reason for the right readers to join your email list
  • Connecting your free content to your paid products or services
  • Planning welcome emails that introduce your business
  • Writing emails that educate, build trust, and invite people to purchase
  • Following up with interested buyers
  • Creating additional sales opportunities for existing customers
You'll know how to continue the conversation after someone leaves your website.
4

Prelaunch your offer

Stop creating products in secret and hoping people buy them when they're finished.

One of the biggest mistakes I see is bloggers spending weeks or months creating an offer before they've built any interest in it. They finish the product. Announce that it's available. Post a link. And hear absolutely nothing. We'll work on a different approach.

  • Research the problems your audience wants solved
  • Test interest in your offer before investing more time
  • Introduce your offer's big idea through content
  • Build anticipation without constantly talking about the product
  • Create a waitlist or interest list when appropriate
  • Prepare your prelaunch emails and content
  • Get your sales page and buying process ready
You'll create a prelaunch campaign with a purpose behind every piece of content.
5

Launch and sell

Now it's time to stop preparing and start inviting people to buy.

We'll develop a launch campaign that fits your offer, audience, and available time.

  • How to announce your offer
  • What to say in your launch emails
  • What to post on Instagram and other relevant platforms
  • How to explain the value of your offer
  • How to address objections
  • How to follow up with interested buyers
  • What to do if sales are slower than expected
  • Which numbers actually matter during a launch
The goal is to make real offers, collect real feedback, and create opportunities for real sales.
6

Improve your sales process and do it again

Your business shouldn't depend on coming up with a brand new idea every month.

Once you've run your campaign, we'll review what happened. What attracted attention? What created interest? Where did people stop moving forward? What questions came up? What sold? And what needs improvement? Then we'll turn those insights into your next action plan.

You'll leave with a sales process you understand and can continue improving.

The part that makes you nervous

But what if you're not good at selling?

Maybe this is the part that makes you nervous. You love creating content. You love teaching people. You love sharing what you know. But asking someone to buy? That feels different.

You don't want to sound desperate. You don't want to annoy your audience. You don't want every Instagram post to feel like an advertisement. And you definitely don't want to turn into someone who talks about making money every five minutes. I get it.

But selling doesn't have to mean pressuring people. It can mean understanding what someone is struggling with, showing her how you can help, and giving her a clear opportunity to purchase. You can be helpful and still sell.

In fact, if you've created something that genuinely solves a problem, you should be able to explain why it matters and invite the right person to buy it. That's a skill. And it's one we're going to practice.
