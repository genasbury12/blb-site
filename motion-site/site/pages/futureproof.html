You keep hearing conflicting opinions.

04 / The old roadmap

You’ve been taught how to build a blog. But have you been taught how to build a business that can survive without one?

For years, the traditional blogging roadmap looked something like this:

Choose a niche.Build a website.Publish helpful content.Rank on Google.Grow your traffic.Monetize through ads, affiliate links and digital products.Repeat.

And while those strategies can still have a place in a business, there are vulnerabilities in building your entire income around them.

What happens when the platform sending you traffic changes its algorithm?
What happens when potential readers receive answers without visiting your website?
What happens when the information you’ve packaged into a digital product becomes easier to access elsewhere?
What happens when someone can use AI to create something remarkably similar to what you’re selling?

If your entire business depends on people needing access to your information, your business may become increasingly vulnerable as information becomes more accessible.

Stop asking only

How do I get more people to read my blog?

And start asking

What makes my business valuable regardless of how people discover it?

That’s a fundamentally different question. And it changes almost everything about how you approach building your business.

Information and value are not the same thing.

AI can

Explain how to start a business.

But understanding those instructions and successfully building a business are two different things.

AI can

Generate a workout plan.

But that doesn’t give someone the accountability, guidance or personalized support they need to follow it.

AI can

Explain how to design a website.

But that doesn’t give you the judgment to look at the website AI gives you and know if it will truly make you money.

AI can generate information about practically any subject. But people may still need someone to help them interpret it, apply it, personalize it, implement it or make important decisions with it. That’s where new opportunities begin to emerge.

Instead of simply asking what information you can sell, you can begin asking:

What problem can I help someone solve more effectively?
What experience can I create for my customers?
What decisions can I help them make?
What work can I help them accomplish?
What can I personalize?
What can I make easier?
What can I deliver in a way that makes the investment worthwhile?

The future of your business doesn’t have to depend on knowing something nobody else knows. It can depend on how effectively you help someone accomplish something they genuinely care about.

10 / Two approaches

Two very different ways to approach the future of blogging.

The traditional approach

  1. Build a blog before establishing the business model.
  2. Prioritize pageviews above customer acquisition.
  3. Monetize attention through ads and affiliate opportunities.
  4. Create products primarily around informational content.
  5. Depend heavily on third party platforms.
  6. React to technology changes as they happen.
  7. Learn individual tactics without understanding the wider business strategy.

The futureproof approach

  1. Establish the business model before building the blog.
  2. Attract potential customers with meaningful buying intent.
  3. Develop offers around valuable customer problems.
  4. Create products and experiences that support customer outcomes.
  5. Build direct relationships and diversify customer acquisition where appropriate.
  6. Evaluate emerging technology for business opportunities.
  7. Develop the strategic skills required to adapt.

You don’t necessarily need to abandon every traditional blogging strategy. You need to understand where each strategy belongs within the business you’re building. And what happens if that strategy stops producing the results you expected.
